Investors often compare mutual funds from different categories as if they are playing the same game. This can mislead investors because each category follows a different framework. The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)[^sebi] sets the rules for each category. These rules state which assets a fund may own, how much of each asset it must hold, and how the manager may use the remaining assets.

A Flexi Cap fund and a Multi Cap fund are both equity schemes, but their market-cap constraints are different. A Dynamic Asset Allocation fund and a Multi Asset Allocation fund are both hybrid schemes. The first manages an equity–debt split, while the second must hold at least three asset classes. Their risk and return profiles can differ even though their names sound similar.

This article compares four categories: the rules each one sets, the portfolios those rules allow, and the risks that follow.

## Key takeaways

Each category sets a core allocation rule and gives the manager a different amount of flexibility:

| Category | Core rule | What the rule leaves to the manager |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexi Cap** | At least 65% of total assets in equity and equity-related instruments | The split across large, mid and small caps, plus the residual allocation |
| **Multi Cap** | At least 75% in equity, including at least 25% each in large, mid and small caps | Stock selection within each market-cap bucket and the remaining 25% |
| **Dynamic Asset Allocation (Balanced Advantage)** | Equity and debt are managed dynamically; there is no fixed equity–debt split | The equity–debt allocation itself, within the scheme's stated framework |
| **Multi Asset Allocation** | At least three asset classes, with at least 10% in each chosen class | Which three asset classes to use and how to allocate the remaining assets |

The practical implication is simple: a fund's category sets the baseline for its risk, return and diversification. Therefore, a return ranking that combines categories may compare different asset mixes rather than the manager's skill.

## Why do mutual fund categories matter?

Equity, debt and alternatives such as commodities, gold, and InvITs have different risk and return profiles. A fund's asset allocation within each asset class can change its behaviour during different market cycles. Clearly defined rules help keep a fund aligned with its stated objectives. Without them, a portfolio could gradually drift from the holdings investors expect.

SEBI updated the mutual-fund categorisation framework in February 2026.[^sebi-circular] For the categories discussed here, the circular gave existing schemes six months to align their names, objectives, strategies and benchmarks where applicable: a deadline of 26 August 2026.[^deadline]

## What does each mutual fund category require?

The table separates what each category must hold from what the manager may hold with the remaining assets.

| Fund category | Scheme type | Core asset exposure | Binding allocation rule | What the residual may hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flexi Cap** | Equity | Equity and equity-related instruments | Minimum 65% of total assets in equity; no mandated split across large, mid or small caps | Equity and related instruments, money-market and other liquid instruments, permitted gold or silver instruments, and InvITs |
| **Multi Cap** | Equity | Equity and equity-related instruments | Minimum 75% in equity, including minimum 25% each in large cap, mid cap and small cap | Equity and related instruments, money-market and other liquid instruments, permitted gold or silver instruments, and InvITs |
| **Dynamic Asset Allocation (Balanced Advantage)** | Hybrid | Equity and debt | No fixed equity–debt split; the allocation is managed dynamically | InvITs, ETCDs, Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs, as permitted by the scheme framework |
| **Multi Asset Allocation** | Hybrid | At least three asset classes | Minimum 10% in each of the three chosen asset classes | InvITs, ETCDs, Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs, as permitted by the scheme framework |

The thresholds above are regulatory constraints, not target allocations. Each fund’s Scheme Information Document may add limits, ranges, or exclusions. Flexibility does not mean unlimited freedom: regulatory and scheme-level limits still apply.

## What portfolios can the rules produce?

The same rules allow very different portfolios. These are the boundaries each category sets, not the holdings of any particular fund.

| Category | Equity can range from | Market-cap mix | Debt and alternatives |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flexi Cap** | 65% to 100% | Any split. A fund can sit almost entirely in large caps, or lean heavily into mid and small caps | Optional, within the residual |
| **Multi Cap** | 75% to 100% | At least 25% each in large, mid and small caps, so at least 50% sits outside large caps | Optional, within the residual |
| **Dynamic Asset Allocation** | No fixed category-level floor or ceiling; set by the scheme's own framework | Not mandated | The equity–debt split moves within the scheme's stated framework |
| **Multi Asset Allocation** | At least 10% if equity is one of the three chosen classes | Not mandated | At least 10% in each chosen class, for example debt and gold |

Two funds in different categories can therefore look alike on one date and still be built on different rules. A Flexi Cap fund holding 90% in large caps and a Multi Cap fund holding the 25% minimum in each bucket are both following their mandates, but their exposure to smaller companies differs by a wide margin.

## What can the residual assets contain?

Residual assets are the part of a portfolio not invested in its core asset classes. This is where differences between equity and hybrid funds become visible.

Flexi Cap and Multi Cap funds may use residual assets for equity-related instruments, money-market and other liquid instruments, permitted gold or silver instruments, and InvITs. Hybrid funds may invest residual assets in InvITs, commodity derivatives (ETCDs)[^etcd], Gold ETFs, and Silver ETFs, subject to the applicable limits.

REITs are counted within equity and equity-related instruments because SEBI reclassified them from 1 January 2026.[^equity-related] InvITs remain classified separately as hybrid instruments.[^invit]

## Category characteristics

A fund's stated benchmark should reflect its category and strategy. The examples below are not mandatory benchmarks for every scheme. Compare a fund with its category peers and stated benchmark, not with whichever index makes its returns look most favourable.

| Category | Characteristics | Example benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexi Cap** | An equity fund where the manager decides the market-cap mix and can change it over time | Nifty 500 TRI[^tri] |
| **Multi Cap** | An equity fund with a fixed minimum in each of large, mid and small caps | Nifty 500 Multicap 50:25:25 TRI |
| **Dynamic Asset Allocation (Balanced Advantage)** | A hybrid fund whose equity–debt split is moved by the manager's model | A hybrid index, such as a 50:50 equity–debt composite |
| **Multi Asset Allocation** | A hybrid fund holding at least three asset classes, often equity, debt and gold | A blended benchmark defined in the scheme's own documents |

## Constraints and risks

| Category | Constraint | Material risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexi Cap** | Minimum 65% equity; no market-cap floor | Equity market risk. The market-cap mix can shift with the manager's view, so the fund's risk profile can change over time without a change in category. |
| **Multi Cap** | Minimum 25% each in large, mid and small caps | Equity market risk, plus the higher volatility and lower liquidity of mid- and small-cap stocks, which the fund must hold even in falling markets for those segments. |
| **Dynamic Asset Allocation (Balanced Advantage)** | No fixed equity–debt split | Equity and interest-rate risk. Results depend on the allocation model; a wrong call can raise equity exposure before a fall or cut it before a rise. |
| **Multi Asset Allocation** | At least three asset classes, minimum 10% each | Risks of every asset class held: equity, interest-rate and credit risk on debt, and commodity price risk on gold, silver or other commodities. |

These descriptions are a starting point, not a substitute for the scheme documents. Each fund's Scheme Information Document sets out its own limits and risk factors, and its riskometer shows the scheme's assessed risk level.[^sources]

## Conclusion: compare like with like

Category rules come before return comparisons. Flexi Cap, Multi Cap, Dynamic Asset Allocation, and Multi Asset Allocation funds can all look diversified while carrying different asset mixes and risks. When you compare funds, compare each one with its category peers and stated benchmark. This helps separate the effect of category rules from the fund manager's skill.

Suitability depends on your goals, time horizon, risk profile and existing portfolio. Category labels alone are not a basis for choosing a fund.

> **Note:** This article is for education, not investment advice, and does not establish that any category suits a particular investor. Review the latest Scheme Information Document and factsheet before investing.
