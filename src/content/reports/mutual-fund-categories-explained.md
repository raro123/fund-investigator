Investors often compare mutual funds from different categories as if they are playing the same game. This can be misleading due to difference between the categories. The Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)[^sebi] defines each category's mandate: which asset classes a fund may own, how much must be allocated to them, and what the manager may do with the balance.

A Flexi Cap fund and a Multi Cap fund are both equity schemes, but their market-cap constraints are different. A Dynamic Asset Allocation fund and a Multi Asset Allocation fund are both hybrid schemes, but one manages an equity–debt split while the other must hold at least three asset classes. Their risk and return profiles can therefore differ even when their names sound similar.

This comparison looks at four categories and four representative funds using portfolio data for 30 June 2026. The aim is to show why category rules matter before comparing returns.

## Key takeaways

Category rules explain much of the portfolio difference:

| Category | Core rule | What the rule leaves to the manager |
|---|---|---|
| **Flexi Cap** | At least 65% of total assets in equity and equity-related instruments | The split across large, mid and small caps, plus the residual allocation |
| **Multi Cap** | At least 75% in equity, including at least 25% each in large, mid and small caps | Stock selection within each market-cap bucket and the remaining 25% |
| **Dynamic Asset Allocation (Balanced Advantage)** | Equity and debt are managed dynamically; there is no fixed equity–debt split | The equity–debt allocation itself, within the scheme's stated framework |
| **Multi Asset Allocation** | At least three asset classes, with at least 10% in each chosen class | Which three asset classes to use and how to allocate the remaining assets |

The practical implication is simple: a fund's category sets the starting conditions for its risk, return and diversification. A one-year return league table that mixes these categories may be ranking different exposures rather than different levels of manager skill.

## Why do mutual fund categories matter?

Equity, debt and alternatives such as commodities, gold and InvITs have different risk and return characteristics. The mix of those assets, and the allocation within each asset class, changes the experience an investor has during both rising and falling markets.

Clear categorisation also keeps a fund's name and stated strategy tied to an enforceable investment boundary. Without those boundaries, a portfolio could gradually move away from the exposure an investor thought they had selected.

SEBI issued a new [Categorization and Rationalization of Mutual Fund Schemes circular](https://www.sebi.gov.in/legal/circulars/feb-2026/categorization-and-rationalization-of-mutual-fund-schemes_99983.html) on 26 February 2026.[^sebi-circular] Existing schemes have until 26 August 2026 to align their names, objectives, strategies and benchmarks with the updated framework.[^deadline]

## What does each mutual fund category require?

| Fund category | Scheme type | Primary asset class | Binding allocation rule | What the residual may hold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flexi Cap** | Equity | Equity and equity-related instruments | Minimum 65% of total assets in equity; no mandated split across large, mid or small caps | Equity and related instruments, money-market and other liquid instruments, permitted gold or silver instruments, and InvITs |
| **Multi Cap** | Equity | Equity and equity-related instruments | Minimum 75% in equity, including minimum 25% each in large cap, mid cap and small cap | Equity and related instruments, money-market and other liquid instruments, permitted gold or silver instruments, and InvITs |
| **Dynamic Asset Allocation (Balanced Advantage)** | Hybrid | Equity and debt | No fixed equity–debt split; the allocation is managed dynamically | InvITs, ETCDs, Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs, as permitted by the scheme framework |
| **Multi Asset Allocation** | Hybrid | At least three asset classes | Minimum 10% in each of the three chosen asset classes | InvITs, ETCDs, Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs, as permitted by the scheme framework |

The thresholds above are regulatory constraints, not target allocations. A scheme's own Scheme Information Document can impose additional limits, ranges or exclusions. “Free to allocate” never means free of all regulatory or scheme-level limits.

## How do four representative funds compare on 30 June 2026?

To make the rules concrete, we compared one fund representing each category. The funds were not selected to declare a winner. They are examples of how different mandates can produce different portfolios on the same date.[^portfolio-data]

| Feature | Parag Parikh Flexi Cap | Nippon India Multi Cap | HDFC Balanced Advantage | ICICI Prudential Multi-Asset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Category** | Flexi Cap (Equity) | Multi Cap (Equity) | Dynamic Asset Allocation (Hybrid) | Multi Asset Allocation (Hybrid) |
| **Binding rule** | Minimum 65% equity | Minimum 75% equity; minimum 25% each in large, mid and small cap | Equity and debt managed dynamically | At least 3 asset classes; minimum 10% each |
| **AUM (₹ crore, 30 June 2026)**[^aum] | 1,43,388 | 54,585 | 1,06,456 | 84,991 |
| **Benchmark** | Nifty 500 TRI[^tri] | Nifty 500 Multicap 50:25:25 TRI | Nifty 50 Hybrid Composite Debt 50:50 TRI | Nifty 200 TRI 65% + Nifty Composite Debt 25% + Gold 6% + Silver 1% + iCOMDEX 3% |

The benchmark row is important. A fund should generally be assessed against the benchmark that reflects its stated mandate, not against whichever index makes the comparison look most favourable.

## What do the four portfolios hold across equity, debt, alternatives and cash?

The equity-versus-hybrid distinction is visible in the allocation snapshot. The two equity schemes held 87.7% and 99.4% in equity, while the two hybrid schemes used their broader mandates to hold debt and alternative assets.

| Fund | Category | Equity | Debt | Alternatives | Cash and equivalents |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund | Flexi Cap | 87.7% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 12.3% |
| Nippon India Multi Cap Fund | Multi Cap | 99.4% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.6% |
| ICICI Prudential Multi-Asset Fund | Multi Asset Allocation | 68.4% | 8.2% | 10.0% | 13.3% |
| HDFC Balanced Advantage Fund | Dynamic Asset Allocation | 72.0% | 25.1% | 0.3% | 2.6% |

*Note: The figures are rounded and should not be read as permanent targets. “Alternatives” includes InvITs and Gold ETF units. REITs are included within equity and equity-related instruments following SEBI's reclassification.[^equity-related] “Cash and equivalents” includes money-market instruments, TREPS and net current assets.[^treps] Debt and cash are shown separately for comparison clarity. Totals may differ from 100% by rounding.*

## Where does market-cap exposure differ across the four funds?

Market-cap exposure is where the Flexi Cap and Multi Cap rules become easiest to see. [^market-cap]

| Fund | Large cap | Mid cap | Small cap | REITs | Foreign equity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund | 65.3% | 3.3% | 4.2% | 4.2% | 10.7% |
| Nippon India Multi Cap Fund | 43.9% | 27.8% | 27.7% | — | — |
| ICICI Prudential Multi-Asset Fund | 45.6% | 14.6% | 6.9% | 1.0% | 0.4% |
| HDFC Balanced Advantage Fund | 52.8% | 9.7% | 8.3% | 1.3% | — |

The Parag Parikh Flexi Cap portfolio had 7.5% across mid and small caps in this snapshot, while the Nippon India Multi Cap portfolio exceeded the 25% minimum in each of its large-, mid- and small-cap buckets. That difference follows directly from the category rules and is not a stylistic preference.

ICICI Multi-Asset Fund satisfies SEBI's 10% floor across all asset classes it invests in (Debt totals 21.5% - including debt in short term money market instruments). Lastly, HDFC Balanced Advantage Fund illustrates dynamic management with its 72.0% equity and 25.1% debt split, which can be freely adjusted as the category enforces no minimum thresholds.

## What can the residual assets contain?

The residual portion is the part of a portfolio not invested in its core asset classes. It is often where the difference between equity and hybrid schemes becomes visible.

Equity schemes such as Flexi Cap and Multi Cap may use the residual for equity-related instruments, money-market and other liquid instruments, permitted gold or silver instruments and InvITs. Hybrid schemes have a more specific residual toolkit that includes InvITs, Exchange Traded Commodity Derivatives (ETCDs)[^etcd], Gold ETFs and Silver ETFs.

In the 30 June 2026 snapshot, Parag Parikh Flexi Cap and Nippon India Multi Cap held most of their residual assets in cash and cash equivalents. HDFC Balanced Advantage held InvIT units, while ICICI Prudential Multi-Asset held a Gold ETF and gold and crude-oil futures in addition to its core equity and debt allocations. InvITs remain classified separately as hybrid instruments, unlike REITs.[^invit]

## Which category suits which investor objective?

No category is automatically better than the others. Each answers a different portfolio question.

| If you want… | Look at | Compare it against |
|---|---|---|
| One equity fund where the manager decides the market-cap mix | Flexi Cap | Nifty 500 TRI and other Flexi Cap funds |
| Required mid- and small-cap exposure without buying three separate funds | Multi Cap | Nifty 500 Multicap 50:25:25 TRI and other Multi Cap funds |
| Equity participation with a manager-controlled equity–debt split | Dynamic Asset Allocation (Balanced Advantage) | Hybrid 50:50-style indices and other Dynamic Asset Allocation funds |
| Equity, debt and commodities such as gold in one product | Multi Asset Allocation | The fund's own blended benchmark and other Multi Asset Allocation funds |

This is a broad framework, not a substitute for reading the scheme documents. Category rules, portfolio allocations, tax treatment and risk can change; compare the current Scheme Information Document and factsheet before acting.[^sources]

## Conclusion: compare like with like

Four funds can all be described as diversified while carrying materially different exposures. One may hold 10.7% in foreign equity, another may be required to hold 27.7% in small caps, a third may move its equity allocation dynamically, and a fourth may hold gold and commodity derivatives.

None of those portfolios is necessarily unusual. Each is operating within its category's rulebook. That is why the first comparison should be between a fund and its category peers, using its own stated benchmark. Only then does a return comparison begin to say something useful about the fund manager rather than the mandate.

Portfolio allocations are snapshots as at 30 June 2026 and can change from month to month. The comparison is educational, is not investment advice, and does not imply that any fund is suitable for a particular investor.
