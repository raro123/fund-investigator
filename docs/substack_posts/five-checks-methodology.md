A headline return can be accurate and still leave the important questions unanswered.

Consider HDFC Flexi Cap Fund – Direct from 1 January 2020 to 10 July 2026. It delivered a 19.7% CAGR against 15.5% for the Nifty 500 TRI. A fixed monthly investment produced a 19.9% IRR against 14.2%. Those figures establish that the fund finished ahead over this period. They do not tell us whether the benchmark was fair, whether the lead was repeated, how much risk was involved, or what an investor experienced during the worst decline.

Fund Investigator uses five checks to separate those questions. The method is a historical screen, not a rating or a recommendation.

## Investigation settings

The worked example uses HDFC Flexi Cap Fund – Direct and the Nifty 500 TRI from 1 January 2020 to 10 July 2026. Fund NAV data comes from AMFI; benchmark data comes from NSE Indices; the calculations are produced in [Deepdive](https://deepdive.fundinvestigator.com/). A fixed SIP amount is assumed at the beginning of each month, and rolling consistency is measured over overlapping three-year periods.

## Check 1: Did the fund beat a fair benchmark?

The first comparison is not fund versus category average or fund versus a convenient index. It is fund versus a benchmark that reflects the scheme’s investment universe. A flexi-cap fund can invest across large-, mid- and small-cap companies, so the broad Nifty 500 TRI is a more defensible reference than a large-cap-only index.

Against that benchmark, HDFC Flexi Cap finished ahead on both measures used here: 19.7% versus 15.5% CAGR for a lump-sum investment, and 19.9% versus 14.2% IRR for the monthly SIP assumption.

That is the point-to-point result. The next check asks whether it depended on one unusually favourable period.

## Check 2: Was the result repeated?

Calendar-year returns reveal whether a lead appeared in different market conditions. Rolling returns test many overlapping start and end dates, reducing dependence on the single date chosen for the headline CAGR.

HDFC Flexi Cap trailed the Nifty 500 TRI in 2020, returning 7% against 18%. It then finished ahead in each of the next five completed calendar years. Across the rolling three-year observations used by Deepdive, it beat the benchmark 59% of the time.

The two facts belong together. Five completed years ahead is evidence of repeatability, but a 59% rolling win rate is not dominance. The selected window includes periods when an investor’s three-year result would still have lagged the index.

## Check 3: Did the return justify the measured risk?

Return alone does not show how widely outcomes fluctuated. We compare annualised volatility and the Sharpe ratio over the same period and with the same assumptions.

The fund recorded 17.5% volatility against 17.8% for the benchmark. Its Sharpe ratio was 0.77 against 0.56. Within this window, the fund therefore produced the higher return with similar measured volatility and more return per unit of that volatility.

The Sharpe ratio is useful, but it is not a complete description of risk. It compresses the path of returns into one number. An investor living through a decline also cares about the depth of the loss and the time spent below the previous peak.

## Checks 4 and 5: How far did it fall, and how long did recovery take?

![Drawdowns for HDFC Flexi Cap Fund and the Nifty 500 TRI, showing maximum declines of 40.2% and 38.1% and recovery times of 256 and 228 days](../../src/assets/images/reports/five-checks-mutual-fund/hdfc-drawdown-recovery.png)

*The return advantage did not remove downside: HDFC Flexi Cap’s maximum drawdown was deeper and its recovery was longer in the period’s worst fall.*

Maximum drawdown measures the deepest peak-to-trough decline. HDFC Flexi Cap fell 40.2% during its worst drawdown in the period, compared with 38.1% for the Nifty 500 TRI. From the trough, the fund took 256 days to regain its previous peak; the benchmark took 228 days.

This is the central tension in the worked example. The fund finished ahead on return and risk-adjusted return, but it did not protect capital better during the most severe decline. Its worst fall was 2.1 percentage points deeper, and recovery took 28 days longer.

The 2020 rebound was also unusually rapid. These figures describe that episode; they are not estimates of how long a future recovery will take.

## Reading the five checks together

The method produces an evidence profile rather than a pass or fail. The sequence matters: each check tests a different claim that the headline return cannot resolve on its own:

- **Benchmark:** the fund finished ahead of a broad, category-relevant index.
- **Consistency:** it led in five of six completed years and 59% of rolling three-year observations.
- **Risk-adjusted return:** the Sharpe ratio was higher while volatility was similar.
- **Drawdown:** the worst decline was slightly deeper than the benchmark’s.
- **Recovery:** the fund remained below its previous peak for 28 more days after the trough.

None of those findings cancels the others. Together they show why “19.7% CAGR” is a starting point rather than a verdict.

## What the evidence does not establish

These checks measure historical outcomes. They do not identify which holdings or portfolio decisions produced them, whether the investment process or management team has changed, how future expenses may affect results, or whether the same pattern will recur.

They also do not determine whether the fund belongs in a particular portfolio. Suitability depends on objectives, time horizon, existing exposures and capacity for loss—questions outside this historical screen.
