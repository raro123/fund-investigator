If you own or are considering Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund, its headline return tells only part of the story. From 1 January 2020 to 13 July 2026, the Direct Growth plan delivered a 20.5% CAGR against 15.5% for the Nifty 500 TRI. But it trailed the index by 10 percentage points in 2022 and remained behind in 2026 year to date. The five checks show whether the longer record was broad enough to survive those exceptions.

## Investigation settings

The analysis covers Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund – Direct Plan – Growth (AMFI code 122639) from 1 January 2020 to 13 July 2026, the latest common date available in Deepdive. The benchmark is the scheme’s declared Nifty 500 TRI. SIP figures assume a fixed investment at the start of each month; rolling returns use overlapping three-year periods.

## The five-check scorecard

**1. Did it beat a fair benchmark?**  
Yes over this analysis window. The fund’s CAGR was 20.5% against 15.5% for the Nifty 500 TRI. A fixed monthly SIP produced a 16.4% IRR against 14.1%. The Nifty 500 TRI is the scheme’s declared broad-market benchmark, although it does not mirror the portfolio’s permitted foreign-equity and debt exposure.

**2. Was the result repeated?**  
The fund finished ahead in five of the six completed calendar years and beat the benchmark in 99% of the rolling three-year observations. It did not lead throughout: it returned -6% in 2022 while the index gained 4%, and in 2026 year to date it was down 4% against the index’s 2% decline at the chart’s rounded precision.

**3. Did the return justify the measured risk?**  
Annualised volatility was 13.7%, compared with 17.8% for the benchmark. The Sharpe ratio was 0.99 against 0.56. Over this period and under the same assumptions, the fund produced more return with less measured volatility.

**4. How far did it fall?**  
The maximum drawdown was -31.2%, compared with -38.1% for the Nifty 500 TRI. The fund’s deepest decline was 6.9 percentage points shallower.

**5. How long did recovery take?**  
The fund regained its previous peak 105 days after its March 2020 trough. The index took 228 days. Recovery was 123 days faster in that episode.

## What stands out

![Annual returns for Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund and the Nifty 500 TRI from 2020 to 2026 year to date, showing the fund ahead in five of six completed years and behind in 2022](../../src/assets/images/reports/ppfas-flexicap-five-checks/ppfas-annual-returns.png)

*The lead appeared across several calendar years, but 2022 shows that the experience was not uninterrupted.*

The strongest evidence is the combination of consistency and risk. A 5.0 percentage-point CAGR lead can sometimes result from one favourable starting date. Here, the fund also led in five completed calendar years and almost every rolling three-year observation. Its lower volatility, higher Sharpe ratio, shallower maximum drawdown and faster recovery all point in the same direction within the selected window.

The counter-evidence is specific rather than incidental. In 2022, the fund lost 6% while the Nifty 500 TRI gained 4%. That ten-point gap is visible precisely because the calendar view does not compress the entire record into one annualised return. The fund was also behind in 2026 year to date. An investor should therefore read the 99% rolling win rate as evidence about overlapping three-year holding periods, not as a claim that the fund beat the index every year or avoided short-term setbacks.

The downside record also needs context. The 2020 market fall and rebound were unusually rapid. A maximum drawdown of -31.2% is shallower than -38.1%, but it still represents a substantial decline from the previous peak. The 105-day recovery describes one historical episode; it is not a forecast for the next one.

## What the evidence does not establish

These checks describe outcomes, not causes. They do not isolate the contribution from Indian equities, foreign equities, debt or individual holdings. They do not attribute results to a particular manager, determine whether the investment process has changed, compare costs with other funds, or predict future performance.

They also do not establish personal suitability. Objectives, time horizon, existing portfolio exposures and capacity for loss require a separate assessment.

## Evidence summary

Across 1 January 2020 to 13 July 2026, Parag Parikh Flexi Cap Fund – Direct led the Nifty 500 TRI on lump-sum return, SIP return, consistency, volatility, Sharpe ratio, maximum drawdown and recovery time. Its 2022 result and 2026 year-to-date position show that the advantage was broad, not uninterrupted.
