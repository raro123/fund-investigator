# Content Philosophy — v2

**Status:** Latest (2026-10-14). Replaces v1 (now `docs/archive/content_philosophy_v1.md`) for the MF distribution pivot.

**Goal:** Establish authority with clients. Every page shows that we know mutual funds, and that this knowledge helps the reader. Content informs first, then persuades. It promotes our services, our advantages and what makes us different.

**Why:** Fund Investigator is a mutual fund distribution business (AMFI-registered MFD). The website is the trust check after a referral. A client with ₹10 lakh–1 crore books a call with a person they believe is an expert. Content earns that belief.

**The Persona:** A registered expert who investigates funds and shows the evidence. Confident about the service, because the process is real. Not a pushy salesperson, not a guru.

## Core Philosophy: Proven Authority

Authority comes from two things: what we know, and what we show.
* **Informative:** Teach something real. The reader leaves with a clearer view of their funds.
* **Persuasive:** Connect that view to our service. Show the problem, then show how we solve it.
* **Proven:** Every claim about us has proof next to it.

**Rule:** Claim, then prove. A strong claim is welcome. An unproven claim is not.

## Fact-Based Storytelling (kept)

We prefer **storytelling based on facts** over fancy claims.
* **The "Fancy Claim" approach:** Tries to create excitement with adjectives ("Incredible growth!").
* **The "Investigator" approach:** Creates engagement by revealing the story inside the data ("The fund stayed flat for 3 years, then suddenly jumped 40% when the benchmark was in bull market").

**Rule:** Let the data provide the drama. Do not manufacture it with hype. This applies to funds and to our own service.

## Two Modes: Promote vs Investigate

Each page type has one mode. Do not mix them.

| Page type | Mode | What we do | Links to the service |
|---|---|---|---|
| Homepage, `/invest/`, `/about/` | **Promote** | Sell the service with confidence. State our differences. Show proof. One gold action per section. | Yes |
| Generic guides (name no fund) | **Teach, then invite** | Teach first. One short line at the end may invite a review call. | Yes, at the end only |
| Fund analyses (name a scheme) | **Investigate** | Evidence only. Authority comes from the quality of the analysis. No promotion. | **Never** (no `/invest/`, WhatsApp or booking links) |

**Why the split:** MFD rules bar return or performance claims about a scheme in promotion. A fund analysis that sells the service turns into a promotion of that scheme.

## Our Differences (message pillars)

Every promotional page draws from these pillars. Use the same words each time.

| Pillar | The claim | The proof we show |
|---|---|---|
| **Analysis and evidence** | We check every fund against a fair benchmark. | Deepdive (the tool we built), our investigations, the sample brief |
| **Portfolio first** | We plan the whole mix first, then the funds. Fewer funds can be the right result. | The review checks: overlap, benchmark check, costs, fit to risk profile |
| **Transparent and personal** | Our commission in writing before you choose. One person from the first call to every review. | The `#costs` section, the ARN, the owner's name and credentials |

Supporting facts: AMFI-registered with ARN; empanelled with every fund house; reviews at regular intervals; the investor approves each transaction by OTP and pays the fund house directly.

## How to Persuade

Do:
- [ ] **Lead with the client's problem**, in their words ("Thousands of funds, and no clear way to know which suit you").
- [ ] **Show the answer as a process**, not a promise ("We check overlap, benchmark, costs and fit").
- [ ] **Name the difference directly.** Contrast with common practice: funds sold on returns, on launches or on commission.
- [ ] **Use proof:** numbers, the sample brief, the review checklist, the ARN, the owner's credentials.
- [ ] **Use the owner's name and expertise** where they add trust (see *Owner name* below). "Expertise" and "expert" are allowed.
- [ ] **End with one clear action** (book a call, or WhatsApp). Say what happens next.
- [ ] **Write benefits in the second person** ("You see your whole portfolio on one page").

Do not:
- [ ] Use exclamation marks.
- [ ] Use fake urgency ("now", "don't miss", "limited").
- [ ] Use unprovable superlatives ("the best", "unmatched"). A comparison with a stated basis is fine.
- [ ] Use fear. State the risk as a fact, then show how we check for it.
- [ ] Interrupt content with a pitch. Ask after value is delivered (no pop-ups or interception modals).

## Legal Limits vs Tone Choices

Legal limits are fixed. Tone choices are ours to change.

**Legal limits (MFD rules — never break):**
- No "advice", "adviser" or "advisory" for the service. Say "help to choose", "suitable", "risk profile", "review".
- No "independent". No "research" as a label for our work. No "financial planning".
- Nothing that plays down risk. No "guaranteed", "safe", "assured returns".
- No return or performance claims about a scheme in promotion.
- Never "free", "zero cost", "no cost to you". Say "No separate fee from you" and explain the commission in the same place.
- No commission rates on the site. We give each client the rates in writing, per scheme, before they invest.
- No AMC names or logos in promotion. Say "every fund house".
- Never name or run down another distributor. Describe the practice only.
- Registered name and ARN appear on every service page (footer covers this).
- Never say "no documents needed" for a portfolio review. The detailed review needs holding details after the first call.

**Owner name (changed 2026-10-20):** the owner holds the AMFI certificate (ARN-370951). Use the name wherever it is relevant.

- Use the name where it adds trust or answers "who do I deal with?". Do not add it to every page.
- The brand leads. The person must not overshadow it.
- The homepage stays brand-only, for privacy. The only exceptions are the footer disclosure and the WhatsApp greeting.
- Claim the registration only: "AMFI Registered Mutual Fund Distributor & SIF Distributor" and the ARN. Never say "adviser" or "analyst".
- Reports carry the owner byline by default. Set `byline: 'brand'` in frontmatter to sign as Fund Investigator.
- A fund analysis that names a scheme: decide the byline case by case. A personal byline can read as named research.
- Link labels may use the name (for example, the report byline links to `/about/#distributor`).

**Tone choices (changed 2026-10-14):**

| Old rule | New rule |
|---|---|
| Not a salesperson; no pitch | Promote the service with confidence on service pages |
| No byline or owner name until SEBI RA | 2026-10-20: name wherever relevant; owner byline on reports; homepage brand-only. Fund analyses: decide case by case |
| Avoid "expertise" | "Expertise" and "expert" are allowed |
| Avoid all superlatives | Avoid unprovable superlatives; proven comparisons are fine |
| Understate when unsure | Prove when unsure. If you cannot prove it, cut it |
| "Partner", "wealth" | Still avoid by default (they imply services we do not offer); not a legal limit |

## Voice & Tone Reference

Think of the site as:
- ✅ An expert who shows the work behind every claim
- ✅ A registered professional who is proud of the process
- ✅ One person the client can call and trust
- ✅ A teacher who makes funds clear

NOT as:
- ❌ A pushy salesperson chasing a sale
- ❌ A guru revealing secrets
- ❌ A lecturer talking down to the reader
- ❌ A neutral bystander who hides the service

## Writing Examples (Show, Don't Tell)

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use | Why? |
|---|---|---|
| "This fund has massive returns." | "The fund outperformed its benchmark by 12%." | Data replaces hype (fund analyses only). |
| "We are the best distributor in India!" | "We check every fund against a fair benchmark, with the tool we built." | A process is proof; a superlative is not. |
| "Free portfolio review." | "No separate fee from you. We earn a commission from the fund house." | Legal limit; transparency is a difference. |
| "Expert financial advice." | "Help to choose funds, from a registered distributor with [credentials]." | No "advice"; credentials prove expertise. |
| "Other distributors cheat you." | "Many funds are sold on returns or commission. We start with your portfolio." | Describe the practice; name no one. |
| "Book now before slots run out!" | "Book a 30-minute call. We look at your whole portfolio first." | No urgency; say what happens next. |

## Frontmatter: which field does what

Three text fields, one job each. Getting these confused is the easiest mistake to make,
because two of them look interchangeable and are not.

| Field | Who reads it | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| `title` | Both | The `<h1>`, the browser tab, the Google result heading, the social card, JSON-LD |
| `description` | **Machines only** | Google snippet, social blurb, JSON-LD, `llms.txt`. **Never rendered on the page.** ~155 chars, written in the words people search |
| `hook` | **Humans only** | The subtitle under the `<h1>`, and the teaser on report cards. Written to read well |

The filename is the URL, so settle it before publishing — renaming a live article breaks its link.

**Full field-by-field reference, with every field annotated:** `docs/templates/report-template.md`.
Copy that file to start a new report. The schema that enforces it is `src/content.config.ts`.

## When Unsure

Ask yourself:
1. "Can I prove this claim on the same page?"
2. "Am I using an adjective where a number or a process would tell a better story?"
3. "Does this page teach the reader something, or only ask for something?"
4. "Is this a fund analysis? If so, does it promote anything?"
5. "Does any word break a legal limit above?"

If you cannot prove a claim, cut it.
