How to Investigate a Mutual Fund: Five Checks

Whether you own a fund or are considering a new one, use these five checks to evaluate its historical performance and risk profile.

Avoiding bad investments is as important as, if not more important than, choosing a promising one. There are thousands of mutual funds in the Indian market. When someone pitches you a fund, it can become overwhelming to decide.

The five checks

  1. Did the fund beat a fair benchmark?
  2. Was the fund's performance consistent, or just a lucky year?
  3. Did the fund's return justify the fund's risk?
  4. How far did the fund fall when the market turned?
  5. How long did the fund take to recover from drawdown?

The first two questions provide insights on a fund's returns and performance consistency. The third one highlights the fund's risk profile to achieve those returns. The combined picture will help you or your advisor assess the fund's suitability in your portfolio.

"The goal of these checks is to move beyond the headline return used in the sales pitch, and to work out which areas deserve a closer look."

Check 1: Did the fund beat a fair benchmark?

A flexi cap manager invests across large, mid, and small-cap companies. Under SEBI's scheme categorisation rules, the benchmark for this category is the Nifty 500 TRI, which covers the same broad opportunity set.

Assessment: the fund beat the benchmark on both lumpsum and SIP returns

Between 1 Jan 2020 and 10 July 2026, the HDFC Flexi Cap Fund outperformed the Nifty 500 TRI on every return measure: 19.7% CAGR against the benchmark's 15.5%, and 19.9% IRR against 14.2%. Total return over the period was 214.4%, against 152.4% for the benchmark.

Check 4 & 5: How far did the fund fall, and how long to recover?

Finally, we examine the worst drawdown during the period and the time taken to recover.

Assessment: a 40% fall in COVID, recovered in about a year

HDFC Flexi Cap fell further than the Nifty 500 TRI at the worst point of the period, -40.2% against -38.1%, both hitting their lows on 23 March 2020. Recovery to the previous peak took 256 days for the fund and 228 days for the benchmark — roughly a year in both cases.

Sharpe ratios use a 6.0% annual risk-free rate, applied consistently to the fund and benchmark.
